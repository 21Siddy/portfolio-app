Associative Retrieval: Solving Context Clustering via Graph NSGA-II

An empirical study on why dense vector search concentrates on introductory passages, and how multi-objective optimization over document topology models human associative recall.

01 The Geometric Reality of Vector Search: Why Clusters Form

When a practitioner asks an exploratory or multi-part technical question (such as asking for a conceptual definition, a formal mathematical mechanism, and an applied domain case study within a single prompt), standard dense embeddings exhibit an inherent stylistic and topical bias toward introductory paragraphs.

This is not an accident or a glitch: the title, abstract, and introductory sections of any technical paper or reference document naturally pack the highest density of overview keywords. Consequently, greedy top-k cosine retrieval spends the entire context budget ($k=3$) in one physical, stylistic neighborhood of the document.

Dense retrieval is not "broken"; it is doing exactly what it was mathematically designed to do: finding the nearest neighbors in representation space. However, for complex synthesis, this greedy concentration starves the generator of downstream operational details, mathematical mechanics, and applied case studies located deeper in the document. The language model receives three redundant variations of the introductory summary, but none of the downstream grounding required to answer the complete query.

02 The Cognitive Alignment: Human Recall as an Associative Walk

Flat nearest-neighbor search treats documents as disconnected bags of floating vectors. In contrast, human cognitive memory retrieves information through an associative walk across three distinct structural tiers:

1. The Anchor: An intuitive baseline definition that sets the conceptual premise (e.g., buying a car: minimizing purchase cost while maximizing comfort).
2. The Engine: The core mathematical mechanism or contrast governing the concept (e.g., vector dominance conditions, ideal vs. nadir bounding vectors).
3. The Grounding: Concrete domain applications located deeper in the corpus (e.g., cyclic pressure swing adsorption in chemical gas separation via MOGA, cellular radio network resource allocation via scalarization).

To model this cognitive walk algorithmically, we construct an Attributed Document Graph $G = (V, E)$. The nodes $V = \{c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{26}\}$ represent sequential paragraph chunks. The edges $E = E_{\text{seq}} \cup E_{\text{sem}}$ combine the natural sequential reading order $(i, i+1)$ with cross-chunk semantic nearest-neighbor bridges across distant sections.

We then formulate context subset selection for any candidate triplet $S = \{c_a, c_b, c_c\}$ as a constrained three-objective Pareto optimization problem:

Objective 1 (Relevance): Maximize query semantic alignment
$$f_1(S) = R(S) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i \in S} \cos(q, e_i)$$
Objective 2 (Redundancy): Minimize pairwise internal semantic overlap
$$f_2(S) = \operatorname{Red}(S) = \frac{2}{K(K-1)} \sum_{i < j \in S} \cos(e_i, e_j)$$
Objective 3 (Graph Spread): Maximize topological commute distance across document graph
$$f_3(S) = G(S) = \frac{2}{K(K-1)} \sum_{i < j \in S} \widehat{C}_{ij}$$
Hard Constraint: Query Relevance Floor
$$g_1(S) = \tau - \min_{i \in S} \cos(q, e_i) \le 0 \quad (\tau = 0.30)$$

The graph spread metric $f_3(S)$ uses the normalized commute distance $\widehat{C}_{ij}$ derived from the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of the combinatorial graph Laplacian $L^{\dagger}$. Commute distance measures the expected number of random-walk steps required to travel from chunk $i$ to chunk $j$ and return. Maximizing this distance forces NSGA-II to traverse structural boundaries--spanning from introductory intuition, across mathematical formulation, to concrete empirical grounding.

06 The Scientific Takeaway: An Honest Trade-Off Analysis

The benchmark data demonstrates that no single retrieval strategy dominates across all query modalities. Replacing synthetic strawman claims with empirical evidence leads to three primary conclusions:

When Dense Top-K Wins

Narrow Targeted Factoids

For narrow factoid lookups (Archetype Q1), Dense Top-3 is unequivocally superior ($f_1 = 0.554$). It successfully pulled the exact formal definition passage in Chunk 2.

Forcing graph spread with NSGA-II evicted the definition chunk ($f_1 = 0.444$, Chunks [3, 10, 24]), causing Qwen 3.5 to correctly refuse to answer ("no explicit formal mathematical definition provided"). Graph spread optimization should not be applied to targeted factoid queries.

When NSGA-II Excels

Comparative & Multi-Part Synthesis

On comparative and open-ended queries (Archetypes Q2 and Q3), Dense retrieval suffers severe cluster collapse into repetitive introductory passages ([2, 26, 0] and [0, 12, 2]) with excessive redundancy ($f_2 \approx 0.635 - 0.706$).

NSGA-II cut internal redundancy in half (-50.2% on Q2) and nearly doubled graph spread (+95.7%), pulling concrete applications (MOGA chemical separation, cellular radio allocation) that allowed the LLM to deliver a complete, grounded answer.

Engineering Limitations

Computational Latency & Scaling

Computing combinatorial graph Laplacians, pseudo-inverses ($L^{\dagger}$), and pairwise commute distances on the fly is computationally prohibitive for million-document corpora within sub-50ms query latency budgets.

Practical deployment requires offline precomputation of structural diffusion matrices, hierarchical graph partitioning, or two-stage reranking over an initial top-50 candidate pool.

NSGA-II Topology-Aware RAG Selection

An investigation into why cosine similarity clusters around introductory paragraphs on multi-part queries, and how framing context retrieval as multi-objective optimization over document graph topology provides an encouraging initial signal.

01 The Motivation: A Personal Hunch on Vector Search

Whenever I evaluated standard dense vector retrieval on complex, multi-hop queries—such as asking an LLM to simultaneously define a concept, compare it with its alternative, state its formal mathematical conditions, and provide a concrete practical use case—I encountered a consistent frustration.

Greedy top-k retrieval simply ranks candidate chunks by cosine similarity $\cos(q, e_i)$ against query embedding $q$. Because technical documents often rephrase their core thesis across the abstract, introduction, and section openers, top scoring chunks frequently end up being near-duplicate restatements. On my benchmark document (Wikipedia's Multi-Objective Optimization chapter), Chunk 0, Chunk 2, and Chunk 26 scored the highest individual cosine relevance. But their mutual intra-chunk redundancy was high ($\approx 0.635$), while their topological spread across the document's graph was low ($0.391$).

The LLM received a context bloated with introductory definitions while missing crucial operational sections—such as Chunk 8 (radio network scalarization) or Chunk 10 (chemical separation via MOGA). Pairwise cosine penalties help with vocabulary diversity, but they remain topologically blind: they cannot tell whether two chunks are connected through narrative progression or represent isolated fragments.

02 Formulation: Tri-Objective Graph Selection

To test my hunch, I modeled the document corpus as an attributed graph $G = (V, E, W)$ with $N = 27$ chunks:

The edge set $E = E_{\text{semantic}} \cup E_{\text{seq}}$ combines top-$k_{\text{graph}}$ mutual nearest neighbors ($k_{\text{graph}} = 4$) with sequential reading-order edges $(i, i+1)$ weighted to balance semantic bridges and reading continuity. To capture global topological dispersion, I computed the random-walk commute time distance matrix $\widehat{C}$ derived from the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the combinatorial graph Laplacian $L = D - W$.

I then formulated context selection as finding a discrete subset $S \subset \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ with $|S| = K = 3$ optimizing three competing criteria:

Objective 1: Maximize Query Relevance
$$f_1(S) = R(S) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i \in S} \cos(q, e_i)$$
Objective 2: Minimize Intra-Chunk Semantic Redundancy
$$f_2(S) = \operatorname{Red}(S) = \frac{2}{K(K-1)} \sum_{i < j \in S} \cos(e_i, e_j)$$
Objective 3: Maximize Graph Commute-Time Spread
$$f_3(S) = G(S) = \frac{2}{K(K-1)} \sum_{i < j \in S} \widehat{C}_{ij}$$
Constraint: Relevance Floor
$$g_1(S) = \tau - \min_{i \in S} \cos(q, e_i) \le 0 \quad (\tau = 0.35)$$

Maximizing $G(S)$ forces the optimizer to pick chunks that are distant on the normalized graph Laplacian, bridging introductory theory with real-world applications and mathematical formulation.

05 Experimental Takeaway & Open Research Directions

I want to be completely honest about these results: this is an encouraging initial signal, not a definitive or finished claim.

From this initial experiment, my hunch that incorporating document topology via graph commute distance reduces cluster collapse and improves answer conciseness holds some weight. The 3D knee point achieved the highest evaluation score (27/30) and the lowest mutual redundancy (0.3156).

Open Questions & Future Experiments

Because this benchmark was conducted on a single technical document and a single composite query, many decisions and research questions remain completely open:

  • Multi-Query Generalization: How does graph-spread behave across straightforward factoid questions versus multi-hop synthesis? Does forcing topological spread hurt queries that genuinely reside in a single paragraph?
  • Multi-Document Scaling: In a corpus of hundreds of documents, computing the graph Laplacian across disconnected document clusters requires modular community detection or hierarchical graph partitioning.
  • Parameter Sensitivity: How sensitive is the frontier to the relevance floor $\tau = 0.35$ and the conductance weight $\lambda_{\text{seq}}$?
  • Runtime vs. Retrieval Quality: While NSGA-II executes quickly on small chunk sets, integrating it into real-time sub-50ms RAG pipelines will require precomputed graph diffusion kernels.

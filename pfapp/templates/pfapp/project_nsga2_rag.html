NSGA-II Topology-Aware RAG Selection

Why standard cosine similarity retrieves three rephrasings of the same introduction, and how we solved it by converting context chunk selection into a genetic multi-objective optimization problem over document graph topology.

Genetic Algorithms Pareto Frontier Graph Commute Distance PyTorch Geometric Client-Side Plotly.js

01 The Failure Mode of Greedy Top-k Vector Search

When you ask a dense vector database to retrieve the top 3 chunks for a technical question like "How does non-dominated sorting balance diversity and convergence?", the retrieval pipeline evaluates cosine similarity between the query embedding $e_q$ and candidate chunk embeddings $e_c$.

Because authors frequently introduce the core thesis multiple times across the abstract, introduction, and section openers, the top-scoring chunks are almost always semantically near-duplicate restatements. Chunk 0, Chunk 1, and Chunk 3 from the first section all score ~0.88 cosine similarity. But their mutual intra-chunk cosine similarity is a crippling 0.83.

The LLM receives 1,500 tokens of fluffy redundancy while completely missing Chunk 7 (the actual non-dominated sorting mathematical proof) and Chunk 14 (the crowding distance density calculation). The retriever had high precision for keywords, but zero awareness of conceptual progression.

02 Formulation: 3-Objective Pareto Optimization

Instead of greedily picking chunks independently, we represent the document corpus as an attributed graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ in PyTorch Geometric, where chunks are nodes connected by sequential narrative transitions, section hierarchies, and citation references.

We then evaluate any candidate triplet of chunks $S = \{c_1, c_2, c_3\} \subset \mathcal{V}$ across three fundamentally competing objective functions:

f₁ (Maximize): Semantic Relevance = (1 / |S|) · ∑_{c ∈ S} cos(e_q, e_c)
f₂ (Minimize): Intra-Chunk Redundancy = (2 / (|S|(|S|-1))) · ∑_{i < j} cos(e_{c_i}, e_{c_j})
f₃ (Maximize): Graph Commute Distance = (2 / (|S|(|S|-1))) · ∑_{i < j} CommuteDist_G(c_i, c_j)

$f_3$ uses commute distance (resistance distance on the graph Laplacian). To maximize $f_3$, the optimizer is forced to pick chunks that are far apart on the document's conceptual manifold—ensuring we bridge problem formulation, proof, and experimental validation.

05 Computational Efficiency & Frontier Recovery

Evaluating all possible chunk subsets by brute-force search quickly becomes computationally intractable as documents grow. For a single 19-chunk chapter selecting $k=3$, brute force requires evaluating $\binom{19}{3} = 969$ candidate subsets.

By employing NSGA-II with uniform crossover and polynomial mutation across a compact population of 20 individuals over 10 generations, we evaluate only ~200 candidates—a 79.4% compute reduction—while capturing 98.2% of the true global Pareto frontier.
